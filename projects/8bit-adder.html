8-bit Custom Adder Design

Overview

The goal of this digital design project was to develop a custom 8-bit adder with improved speed compared to a synthesized ripple carry adder (RCA). Efficient adder design is essential for high-performance computing systems, as adders are core components of arithmetic logic units (ALUs) and directly impact computational speed.

Design Approach

While the RCA is known for its simplicity, its serial carry propagation leads to high delay. To improve speed, we implemented a hybrid architecture combining a carry-lookahead adder (CLA) with carry-skip logic. This structure allowed local parallel carry calculations and faster bypassing of unnecessary delays.

The final architecture consisted of:

  • Two 3-bit carry-lookahead adders (CLA)
  • Two half adders
  • Carry-skip logic to bridge the CLA blocks

This hybrid approach used fewer transistors and less power than a full 8-bit CLA, offering a practical trade-off between speed and hardware complexity.

Optimization & Results

Some transistor-level optimizations were explored to reduce delay and power usage. While they slightly reduced power consumption, they didn’t lead to significant increases in maximum operating frequency compared to using standard cells. The final design met all timing requirements and operated successfully at over 2.4 GHz.
